# QuisqueyaTech — visual direction explorations

These previews exist to compare visual structure before choosing a production homepage. They deliberately preserve the current marketing-site boundary and SEO architecture.

Preview routes:

- `/preview/a` — Growth Agency
- `/preview/b` — Premium Systems Consultancy
- `/preview/c` — Tech + Motion
- `/preview/d` — Local Business Growth

The previews are `noindex` and are intentionally excluded from the sitemap.

## Shared principles

All four directions keep the same fundamentals:

- QuisqueyaTech remains a business-systems and technology consultancy rather than an AI-assessment product.
- Public conversion is phone/contact first.
- Services remain Web + SEO, Automation, Applied AI, and Software/Integrations.
- Case studies use existing verified project context; no fabricated ROI, testimonials, review counts, or performance metrics.
- Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.
- The visual layer can change without undoing translated slugs, canonicals, hreflang, sitemap, structured data, or the public marketing-only scope.

## A — Growth Agency

Purpose: maximize commercial clarity and scanability.

Structural influence: the conversion rhythm commonly seen in strong US web agencies, especially the sequence used by iCreate Your Site: clear hero, trust/proof immediately below, service cards, visible work, reasons to choose the firm, process, and repeated contact opportunities.

QuisqueyaTech adaptation:

- does not claim unverified website counts, ratings, rankings, or revenue;
- uses selected-work names as real proof instead of fake logo walls;
- keeps the broader systems/automation/AI positioning;
- uses business imagery, browser-like project visuals, cards, and short copy blocks;
- keeps direct phone contact as the main CTA.

Best if the primary goal is making the company easy to understand and easy to hire.

## B — Premium Systems Consultancy

Purpose: increase perceived strategic value and reduce the feeling of a commodity web agency.

Characteristics:

- dark editorial hero;
- larger typography and more whitespace;
- fewer cards above the fold;
- capabilities presented as a system rather than a menu of products;
- project narratives given more room;
- consultancy/process language is more prominent than technology labels.

Best if QuisqueyaTech wants to move toward higher-value business-systems engagements and custom implementations.

## C — Tech + Motion

Purpose: recover the visual energy of the original `main` experience without restoring the Assessment Platform as the product.

Characteristics:

- reuses the existing KineticText, DawnSweep, SystemFlow, MotionCard, reveal, stagger, and scroll-progress language;
- presents services as interactive modules;
- adds a visual system map instead of another long explanatory section;
- uses stronger gradients, depth, motion, and technical UI motifs;
- keeps the business message ahead of the technology.

Primary risk: becoming a generic AI/startup aesthetic. The direction should only be chosen if the final refinement continues to show real business context and case studies.

## D — Local Business Growth

Purpose: speak directly to clinics, home services, professional services, and other SMBs whose revenue depends on response time and follow-up.

Structure follows the customer journey:

1. Find the business.
2. Contact the business.
3. Reach a concrete next step.
4. Measure and follow up.

Characteristics:

- warmer imagery;
- simpler language;
- industry/use-case sections;
- phone CTA is highly visible;
- services are framed as the missing layer between marketing and operations.

Best if the immediate go-to-market strategy centers on local-service SMBs in the Dominican Republic and United States.

## Recommendation for evaluation

Do not choose based on the hero alone. Review each preview on desktop and mobile and score it on:

1. Can a business owner understand what QuisqueyaTech does in under 10 seconds?
2. Is the next action obvious?
3. Does the page demonstrate enough capability without becoming text-heavy?
4. Do the case studies feel important rather than decorative?
5. Does the design feel specific to QuisqueyaTech rather than a generic template?
6. Would the structure still work after the final bilingual copy is refined?
7. Can it support future real proof—results, client logos, screenshots, reports—without redesigning the whole page?

The final production direction can combine sections from multiple previews. These are design systems to compare, not four mutually exclusive brands.
